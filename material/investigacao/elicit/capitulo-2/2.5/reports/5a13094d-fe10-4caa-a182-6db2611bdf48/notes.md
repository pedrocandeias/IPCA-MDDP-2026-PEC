## Hierarchical Definition Framework

The most consistent definition across the literature presents AI as a nested hierarchy :

**Artificial Intelligence (AI)** is "an umbrella term that relates to machines with the ability to perceive, synthesise, and infer information" . More comprehensively, AI represents "a family of computational techniques that approximate cognitive functions such as learning, reasoning, and pattern recognition through data-driven models" .

**Machine Learning** is "a sub-discipline of Artificial Intelligence where algorithms are trained with examples to make predictions" .

**Deep Learning** "refers to the creation of artificial neural networks where inputs are processed in various layers of neurons trying to mimic the learning process of the human brain" .

**Generative AI** is "a rather new concept that uses deep learning algorithms to generate a creative output (e.g., image, text, video, sound) based on a given input (so-called 'prompt')" . It can also be defined as "a class of AI systems capable of autonomously generating new content such as images, texts, and design solutions based on learned data patterns" .

## Key Characteristics for Design Context

The literature consistently emphasizes several important characteristics:

1. **Cognitive Function Approximation**: AI systems approximate human cognitive functions like learning and pattern recognition, but through computational rather than biological processes .

2. **Data-Driven Learning**: All forms of AI rely on learning from data examples rather than being explicitly programmed for every scenario .

3. **Creative Output Generation**: Generative AI specifically creates new content rather than just analyzing existing data, making it particularly relevant for design applications .

As stated: "AI does not replace human-centered design; rather, it tends to amplify and accelerate it by augmenting human creativity and decision-making with data-driven intelligence" . This positioning is reinforced by multiple sources emphasizing that "AI serves as a supportive tool in the design process, facilitating designers in creating unique designs, saving time, and reducing effort" .

This hierarchical definition framework, combined with the consistent emphasis on AI as an assistive rather than replacement technology, provides the most academically robust foundation for introducing AI concepts to non-specialist readers in industrial design contexts.
