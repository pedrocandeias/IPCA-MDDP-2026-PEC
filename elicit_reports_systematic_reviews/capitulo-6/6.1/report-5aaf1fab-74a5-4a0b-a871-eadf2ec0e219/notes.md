## Query
How is artificial intelligence being integrated into upper-limb prosthetic customization systems that combine anthropometric input, explicit parametric CAD models, AI-based parameter suggestion, clinician oversight, and 3D-printing workflows?

## Objective
Support section 6.1 by grounding the role of AI as an assistive layer inside a parametric prosthetic customization workflow.

## Why it matters for the manuscript
The report is useful because it does not simply confirm that AI is present in prosthetics; it identifies a structural gap between current AI uses and the fully integrated workflow proposed in the project. This supports a more rigorous framing of the system as forward-looking but grounded in real research gaps.

## Key findings

- No reviewed study covered all five target components together:
  - anthropometric input
  - parametric CAD models
  - AI parameter suggestion
  - clinician oversight
  - 3D-printing workflow
- Current literature is fragmented across subsystems rather than integrated workflows.
- AI is used predominantly for:
  - biosignal interpretation
  - motor control
  - gesture recognition
  - adaptive control
- The path from anthropometric capture to AI-suggested parametric CAD modification is poorly documented.
- Clinician oversight is largely absent in the reviewed systems, which is a critical gap relative to the proposed project.
- The report supports a strong distinction between:
  - AI for prosthetic control
  - AI for prosthetic customization and parameter suggestion
- The project’s architecture is therefore defensible as a distinct contribution:
  - explicit parametric model
  - anthropometric input
  - server-side AI suggestion
  - human review before fabrication
  - local rendering and export

## Key limitations

- The report itself notes that the evidence base is fragmented.
- Much of the literature is stronger on control systems than on design customization.
- Some included studies have incomplete full-text access.
- The absence of integrated workflows in the literature means the report is more valuable for identifying gaps than for validating a mature established model.
